# Common krait

The common krait, *Bungarus caeruleus*, is a highly venomous elapid. It is black or bluish black, with narrow pale bands. Adults are often about a metre long. It lives in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and Nepal, in grassland, fields, and around houses.

## How people recognise it

The bands are thin and pale on a dark body. The scales along the spine are enlarged and hexagonal, which is a mark of kraits. A wolf snake can also show pale bands. It does not have that enlarged vertebral row, and it is more often seen moving by day around houses.

The common krait is mostly active at night. A daytime photo of a banded snake near a house is a reason to look again, not a reason to force a name.

## What a name in this app does not prove

The small model inside the app includes the common krait. The wide 135-species catalog does not. If the wide catalog is selected, it can offer a different banded snake. That gap is a limit of the catalog, not a fact about the animal.

A score is not a medical finding.
