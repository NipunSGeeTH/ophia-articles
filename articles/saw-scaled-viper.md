# Saw-scaled viper

*Echis carinatus* is a small venomous viper of the Indian subcontinent, with related populations in parts of the Middle East and Central Asia. It is the smallest of the four snakes most often named in bite records from India. It is easy to overlook because it is small and well camouflaged.

## How people recognise it

The body is short and the scales are strongly keeled. When alarmed it can rub those scales together, which makes a rasping sound. That sound is not in a photograph. On a photo, look for a small, stout viper with a zigzag or arrow-like mark and a vertical pupil.

Do not pick it up to hear the rasp or to see the head. The head marks are only useful from a safe distance.

## Where it lives

It uses dry and open country, scrub, and the edges of farms. It is a ground snake.

## What a name in this app does not prove

The quick model includes a saw-scaled viper class. A score is a photograph match. It is not a medical finding.
