# Russell's viper

Russell's viper, *Daboia russelii*, is a highly venomous viper native to South Asia. It was described in 1797 and named after Patrick Russell. It is one of the four snakes most often named in bite records from the region.

## How people recognise it

The body is stout. The pattern is a chain of oval spots along the back. The head is triangular, and the pupil is a vertical slit. Those marks are easier to see in a photo of the whole animal than in a tight crop of scales.

A python can also look blotched. Its marks are less regular, and the head shape is different. If both names seem possible, keep both and do not treat the top score as settled.

## Where it lives

It lives across much of South Asia, including farmland and open country. It is often on the ground rather than in trees.

## What a name in this app does not prove

Both models in the app can suggest this species. The score is a match to a photograph. It is not a diagnosis, and it does not describe treatment.
