# King cobra

The king cobra is a group of large venomous snakes in the genus *Ophiophagus*, native to Asia. Despite the common name, it is not a true cobra in the genus *Naja*. It is the world's longest venomous snake. Adults are often around three to four metres, and the longest measured individuals are longer still. It occurs from the Indian subcontinent through Southeast Asia to southern China, and it is widespread without being commonly seen.

## How people recognise it

It can spread a hood, so a distant photo is easy to confuse with a spectacled or monocled cobra. The king cobra is much larger, the head is longer, and the hood lacks the spectacle mark of *Naja naja*. Size is hard to judge unless the photo includes the ground, a branch, or the whole animal.

It feeds largely on other snakes, which is what the genus name refers to.

## What a name in this app does not prove

Both models can suggest a king cobra. A large hooded snake and a smaller true cobra are a known mix-up. If the animal is not obviously very long, do not lock the name from one photo.

A score is not a diagnosis.
